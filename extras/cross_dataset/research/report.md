# Raw qubit readout traces exist beyond Lienhard

**Yes: as of 6 November 2026, at least six superconducting deposits hold real, per-shot, time-resolved readout records with state labels that anyone can download, three of them confirmed by more than one researcher, and other platforms add more.** The closest substitute for dispersive transmon IQ readout is the QICK dataset of Di Guglielmo et al. on Zenodo: 1,000,000 labelled shots of 770 I/Q pairs over 2.5 µs, under CC-BY ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/14427490)). The second is the Hatridge-lab deposit on Harvard Dataverse, 310 GB of 80-sample I/Q records with g, e and f preparation, whose labels need work before use ([Dataverse](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/DPMID3)). Neither replaces Lienhard for multiplexing or crosstalk, because every public superconducting trace set anyone opened records a single qubit's readout, so far as the notes could verify. The draft's "no other raw-trace dataset is publicly available" is therefore false as written, and it also implies that Lienhard is public, which Oraqle's README denies ([README](https://github.com/manosgior/Oraqle)). "Commercial cloud providers do not release raw readout traces publicly" holds only when narrowed to published datasets from providers' cloud QPUs. Corrected wording for both follows below. The claim that survives, that no labelled, multiplexed, multi-qubit superconducting trace set is public, rests on weak search and on checks only a browser can finish, above all a Purdue SharePoint link described as holding two-qubit multiplexed traces.

## Two deposits can stand in for dispersive transmon traces today

The notes grade records on a six-tier scale used throughout this report. **T1** means real hardware, per-shot, time-resolved, downloadable now, with labels present or derivable. **T2** is the same with labels missing or unclear, an undocumented format, or very few shots. **T3** is per-shot but integrated to one value, **T4** has a time axis but is averaged, **T5** is simulated, and **T6** is not obtainable (on request, dead link, code only). Time-resolved records come in two kinds, and the difference decides whether a truncation sweep is possible: a **waveform** has samples inside one readout pulse, and a **monitor** is a stream of consecutive short integrations. In the confirmation columns, "arrays" means a researcher opened the array or read its file header, and "docs" means the evidence is documentation or file names alone. A researcher counts only if their note records their own read. Nobody downloaded a large deposit in full or trained a model on one, so "yes" under download means byte-range or sample reads succeeded without a login.

Both usable deposits read one qubit, so they can replace Lienhard for per-qubit readout and discriminator studies, not for multiplexing or crosstalk.

**Di Guglielmo et al. is the only deposit that is both large and ready for a discriminator benchmark.** Its four `.npy` files hold `X_train` (900000, 1540), `X_test` (100000, 1540) and matching label vectors, and those shapes account for the record's 12,328,000,512 bytes exactly, by my arithmetic with 128-byte headers ([Zenodo API](https://zenodo.org/api/records/14427490)). One transmon on a frequency-tunable multi-transmon chip is read through QICK on a ZCU216, with ADCs at 6.88 GS/s demodulated on the FPGA and 500,000 shots per prepared state ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14663)). The columns interleave I and Q, since the repository's script asserts a width of `770*2` and slices by `*2` ([script](https://github.com/fastmachinelearning/ml-quantum-readout/blob/dev/data/qick_data/generate_window.py)). Rows are shots, not averages: a 500-row sample has a per-sample standard deviation near 825 ADC units, matching the single-shot scale in the paper's Fig. 3a. The 2.5 µs window is long enough to repeat the draft's truncation sweep (chapters/qubit_readout.tex:47), and the paper reports **96% single-shot fidelity** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14663)).

Three practical traps remain. Labels are block-ordered, with the change at index 450,000 in train and 50,000 in test, so shuffle before subsetting. The repository's data README describes an older version (909,000 rows, width 1,440, 2.6 ns), so trust the paper for these files ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fastmachinelearning/ml-quantum-readout/dev/data/README.md)). The Zenodo description is empty, so the paper is the documentation. Preparation is well characterised: cooling brings thermal population from about 6% to below 1.6%, and π-pulse error is at most 0.4%. Others already treat the data as public. LUNA calls it "the publicly available superconducting-qubit readout dataset" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07808)), SNAC-Pack 2.0 uses it ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16138)), and a third-party archive states "The readout data are public" ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/23777889)). **The draft itself cites this paper** as `Guglielmo2025End` (chapters/intro.tex:34, chapters/related_work.tex:4), and that paper says "we have made our dataset available on Zenodo", so a reviewer can find the counterexample in the draft's own references.

**Cao et al. is the richer set and the harder one.** The Dataverse file API redirects to S3 and serves byte ranges without a login ([API](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/api/datasets/:persistentId/?persistentId=doi:10.7910/DVN/DPMID3)). Range-read headers show `I_data` and `Q_data` as float64 (220000, 80) in [a natural-decay file](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/api/access/datafile/13103194) and (140000, 80) in [a pump file](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/api/access/datafile/13102181). The [deposited notebook](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/api/access/datafile/13099454) averages `raw_I_data[:, 20:80]` under the comment "the readout signal integration window is from index 20 to index 80", and its docstring says each record holds "a first measurement for preparing state" and a second measurement after the pump, with rows alternating between them. A 220,000-row file therefore holds 110,000 measurement pairs. File sizes imply about 240 million rows in total, or about 120 million pairs (inference, since most files were not opened). The rows are shots: the standard deviation across rows at sample 50 was 10.4, against 0.27 expected for noise on a mean.

Three things stand between this deposit and a benchmark. There is no README, and nobody found the sample period or readout duration; the paper's 1.0 GSa/s figure is the AWG, not the digitizer ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21765)). Labels sit in file names rather than rows, and the second measurement's label holds only at short delays because a variable wait or pump precedes it. And **label quality is unverified**: in the first 1,500 rows, `prep_e` and `prep_g` files had similar integrated I (3.23 and 3.47), though that check did not separate herald rows. The repository note still judged this the closest public match for leakage work, because it pairs g, e and f preparation with time-resolved per-shot I/Q. Validate the labels first.

**Koolstra et al. is real transmon data for a different task.** The arXiv text still says a data link "will be available here later" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12023)), and only the [GitHub README](https://github.com/qnl/trajectories_lstm) points to the Drive folder. Files are stored as `prep_g/meas_{X,Y,Z}/t_{0..50}/`, and the 5 µs slice holds `I_binned` (2357, 126) at 40 ns, filtered copies at 20 ns and `final_ro_results` of 0s and 1s. These are binned records from a 1 GS/s digitizer, not raw ADC samples. The natural task is trajectory reconstruction under a Rabi drive, not projective state discrimination. Only one researcher reached it, which I read as structural: repository searches cannot see a Drive folder.

**Gunyhó et al. is the most thoroughly confirmed record and the weakest training set.** Four researchers separately read `bolometer-characterization-timetrace-76.nc` and found `readout_meta_CH0_re` and `_im` at (2, 1000, 781). The paper says it records "the full time traces of the bolometer output signal for 1000 single shots" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03668)). The detector is a thermal one rather than a dispersive amplifier chain, and the paper's best fidelity is 0.618 ([paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41928-024-01147-7)). The rest of the zip does not raise the count: `timetrace-77` is (2, 10000) integrated shots, and the larger `qubit-bolometer-readout-*` files look averaged.

**Hassani et al. adds small labelled waveforms from a different qubit**, and its record title gives no hint of them. The deposited `QND_ness.nb` sums each trace and histograms the sums, and single rows differ visibly from the row average, but nobody ruled out per-row hardware averaging from instrument metadata. Fig. 7a of the same deposit adds 40 files of 40,000 single-shot I/Q bins with g and e labels, which are T3.

**Monitor records stop short of a readout waveform.** Each Spiecker sample is a separate 128 ns measurement ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00499)), and the Bargerbos, Pita-Vidal and Sett streams are unlabelled, so they support state tracking and jump analysis but no truncation sweep inside a pulse. Bargerbos calls its records "2 s time traces" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12754)). One lookalike should not be counted: `malab_{e,g}_state_A_fpga.csv` in the QICK repository has 5,000 rows of 1,540 columns, but its README calls them "Mock readout data", and whether a qubit was involved is not verified ([README](https://github.com/fastmachinelearning/ml-quantum-readout/blob/dev/data/fpga_testing/README.md)).

**Multi-qubit data exists only after integration.** Caune et al. store complex `soft_measurements` per qubit and round from Rigetti Ankaa-2, (100000, 28) in `stability_9`, with no per-shot prepared state in stability runs ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/15364358)) (2 researchers, arrays). van der Meer et al. hold integrated I and Q voltages per qubit with calibration points from a 17-qubit transmon processor ([4TU](https://data.4tu.nl/datasets/886d8394-adfe-4560-807c-c0fa007110df)) (1 researcher, arrays; 1 more, docs). The FCM repository holds 8,192-shot complex I/Q from four IBM devices, with (8192, 127) calibration files labelled by name ([GitHub](https://github.com/Quasar-UniNA/FCM-based-QubitDiscriminator)) (2 researchers, arrays). Cao, Akhouri et al. cover up to five qubits by file name ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/22719098)) (3 researchers, arrays). These can test a one-point discriminator or inter-qubit correlations, not a trace model.

**Labelled single-qubit integrated sets are plentiful.** Zwanenburg et al. give 65,536 fluxonium shots per state per file ([4TU](https://data.4tu.nl/datasets/22122209-2f78-4a66-8d04-fe18ac8b244e)) and Stefanski gives 10,000 per state ([4TU](https://data.4tu.nl/datasets/956b028c-6ea2-402a-9f68-fa3a5eb0e068)) (1 researcher each, arrays). Zheng et al. 2026 sweep readout duration on a fluxonium and a transmon with g, e and f calibration, which makes it the integrated set most relevant to duration trade-offs, though its README warns that fluxonium field names are inverted ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/23367046)) (1 researcher, arrays; 1 more, docs). Beaulieu et al. ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/20825189)) and Arnold et al. ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/14033026)) add 25,000 and 50,000 shots per state (1 researcher each, arrays).

Bits and simulation do not fill the gap. Google Quantum AI's QEC deposits hold `measurements.b8`, detection events and decoder outputs, never analog values, and the README says detection events can be rederived from the bits ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/6804040)). AlphaQubit's analog-looking tensors are Pauli+ simulation ([README](https://storage.googleapis.com/gdm-qec/README)). The cloud note found no downloadable simulated set of realistic dispersive traces, and QDataSet has a dead link and holds Pauli measurement distributions ([README](https://github.com/eperrier/QDataSet)).

Signals are the main barrier. **Spin traces are current or voltage telegraph blips at 7.6 to 200 kHz**, and only Zheng 2019 records magnitude and phase, at 1 MHz. Parity deposits are complex-valued and so look most like IQ, but they are continuous telegraph records with no per-shot preparation, so readout error can only be inferred from a model. Mądzik is the only non-superconducting T1: its script says records 81 to 120 show a spin-up electron and records 121 to 160 mostly show spin-down ([file](https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/26691152)). Struck's paper says "on reasonable request" even though the data is on Zenodo, and its 10⁶ labels inherit the Bayesian filter's errors ([paper](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8355192/fullTextXML)). Krzywda's record says "single-shot readout traces", but two researchers read one value per repetition, so it is T3 ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/18470200)).

Ions are the clearest gap. **No note found public time-resolved ion readout**, even though Seif et al. describe exactly that data type, with photon arrival times on PMT channels and 80,000 samples per label ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.07718)). The 27,000 Hugging Face fluorescence frames have no verified hardware origin ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/anonymousshare/300-Qubits-Register-Dataset)) (2 researchers, docs only). Reuse carries hazards too: the Struck, Kelly, Chiu, Neyens and Philips files need unpickling, which should happen in isolation, and Xue 2022 forbids reuse without approval despite its CC-BY tag ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/5044450)).

## Neither draft sentence survives as written

### "No other raw-trace dataset" is false, and it implies Lienhard is public

The scope table at chapters/intro.tex:66 reads "The experimentally-extracted IQ trace dataset of Lienhard et al. (no other raw-trace dataset is publicly available)". **All five notes reject the sentence as written.** It fails on two independent grounds. First, the counterexamples above are public, labelled and per-shot, and a charitable reading that "raw-trace dataset" means multiplexed multi-qubit traces is not what the sentence says. Second, "no other" tells the reader that Lienhard's data is itself public, while Oraqle's README says "The dataset is **not public and is not redistributed here**" ([README](https://github.com/manosgior/Oraqle)) and its dataset note says "not publicly downloadable" and "no Zenodo/figshare/GitHub release exists" ([dataset README](https://github.com/manosgior/Oraqle/blob/main/dataset/README.md)). HERQULES confirms the route: it used data "provided by the authors" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03895)), and its repository says "Feel free to shoot me an email for dataset access" ([GitHub](https://github.com/satvikmaurya/HERQULES)). On this scale Lienhard is T6, so **the draft's own dataset is less public than the deposits its sentence says do not exist.**

What survives is narrower. Four notes found no public, per-shot, time-resolved, multi-qubit superconducting record, and the platform note explicitly leaves that question to them. Corrected wording for the table cell:

```latex
The experimentally-extracted five-qubit, frequency-multiplexed IQ trace dataset of
Lienhard et al.~\cite{Lienhard2022Reinforcement}, obtained from its authors (public
superconducting raw-trace datasets exist, e.g.,~\cite{Guglielmo2025End}, but to our
knowledge, as of Nov.~2026, none offers labeled multiplexed multi-qubit readout)
```

Run the SharePoint check below before relying on the last clause. Separately, `Lienhard2022Reinforcement` is an APS abstract on reinforcement-learning pulse shaping, while the dataset README traces the data to the PR Applied paper, `Lienhard2022Deep`, so the cite key deserves a look.

The core holds narrowly: the cloud note found **no provider-released trace dataset and no published time-resolved per-shot traces collected through any commercial cloud route**. That finding rests on a single researcher, who did not enumerate provider organisations in full. The sentence still misleads, because providers differ and the routes have changed:

Three further problems sit in the sentence. First, an unqualified "providers do not release traces" is contradicted by **Microsoft, which runs Azure Quantum and has published raw parity time traces** ([GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/azure-quantum-parity-readout)), though from research devices and not cloud QPUs. The cloud note's suggested wording misses this, so the rewrite below adds "from its cloud QPUs". Second, third-party dumps of integrated IBM data use the word "raw", and these must stay distinct from traces: Indahl sets `meas_type = 'kerneled'` and calls the result "a raw IQ point" ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/23812756)), and Lee et al. deposit "raw IQ readout data" in unopened pickles ([Figshare](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.32539992)) (1 researcher each, docs only). Third, the logic and the ending both fail. Provider policy no longer explains the choice of Lienhard once public sets exist; the stronger reason is that models such as the Baseline FNN take frequency-multiplexed input (chapters/overview.tex:60). "All SOTA" is also too strong: the discriminator note counts about 20 papers on own-lab hardware against 11 confirmed Lienhard reuses and two reuses of Di Guglielmo, and QubiCML, one of the draft's six models, used LBNL data ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18807)). Corrected wording:

```latex
We know of no commercial cloud provider that publishes time-resolved single-shot traces
from its cloud QPUs: IBM's current interface exposes at most integrated per-shot I/Q
points and pulse-level control was removed in February 2025, Amazon Braket's
\texttt{capture\_v0} returns a bit, and Rigetti's documented \texttt{RAW-CAPTURE} leaves
collection to the user. Public single-qubit trace datasets exist~\cite{Guglielmo2025End},
but they cannot exercise frequency-multiplexed discriminators, so we conduct all readout-
and discriminator-level experiments on the five-qubit dataset of Lienhard et
al.~\cite{Lienhard2022Reinforcement}, which we obtained from its authors and which
HERQULES, KLiNQ and MCMit also use~\cite{Maurya2023Scaling,Guo2025Klinq,Giortamis2025Mcmit}.
```

If space is tight, drop the clause after the first colon. Both original sentences already appear in arXiv v1 (Table 1 and §3.3) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01939v1)), so a corrected version there is worth considering. `Guglielmo2025End` is already in references.bib; citing Cao, Gunyhó or Koolstra needs new entries.

Four negatives carry the most weight here: no multiplexed public traces, no Lienhard re-host, no cloud-collected traces and no time-resolved ion data. Each is an absence of hits, not proof. **GitHub code search was unavailable**, because it needs authentication, `gh` was not installed, `grep.app` returned 429 and Sourcegraph often timed out. Repository search covers only names and descriptions, ANDs its words and then hit the rate limit, so a dump in a poorly described repository cannot be excluded. An authenticated search for `DRaw_C_Tr_v0-001`, `DRaw_C_Te_v0-002`, `meas_level=0` and `RAW-CAPTURE` is the cheapest check still open. Web searches for those Lienhard file names found no host, but that reaches indexed pages only.

**Keyword recall is poor.** Zenodo treats unquoted words as OR, so `qubit readout traces` returned 30,471 hits, and only the top 50 to 100 per query were reviewed ([Zenodo API](https://zenodo.org/api/records?q=qubit+readout+traces)). The phrases closest to the need, such as "readout transients" and "discriminator dataset", return zero. The best deposits were invisible to keywords: Cao's record mentions only "data and processing code", Zwerver's title is "Data and analysis", and Mądzik surfaced only through its paper's data statement. 4TU's native search returned 0 or its cap of 100, Mendeley's returned unrelated records and ETH's refused access, so coverage there rests on DataCite metadata, which often lacks file detail.

**Paper-side and regional coverage is thin.** Paywalled APS, ACM and IEEE full texts went unread, so many "no statement" findings come from arXiv text and could differ in the journal version. Google Scholar was not accessed, Semantic Scholar and the arXiv API rate-limited, OpenAlex ran about 15 of 22 planned queries, and citers were screened by title. USTC, Science Data Bank and Tencent deposits got little beyond DataCite, and ETH's repository could not be searched directly. On the cloud side, Pasqal, Quantinuum, QuEra and AQT documents were not read, `docs.rigetti.com` returned only a title, and nobody created an account or ran a job, so capability statements rest on documentation. Finally, sampling limits every "no time axis" verdict: van der Meer's rests on an experiment-name census and one opened file among 1,065 datasets, and small figure deposits were judged from listings. The platform note ranks its ion negative as its least certain.

## Conclusion

The draft's argument gets stronger once it stops claiming that traces are scarce. What sets Lienhard apart is labelled frequency multiplexing, which is a better reason to use it than the one the draft gives. The uncomfortable corollary is reproducibility: every readout and discriminator number comes from data reviewers cannot download, while a 12 GB public set sits in the draft's own reference list. Repeating the truncation sweep and per-qubit discriminators on the QICK traces would give a public cross-check on a second device, and it would make the abstract's plural "datasets" (chapters/abstract.tex:15) true. The Hatridge-lab set could extend that check to leakage once its labels are validated. Crosstalk claims would stay tied to Lienhard.

Any uniqueness claim here has a short life, so the sentence should carry its date. Many of the richest deposits date from 2025 and 2026, and 2026 alone added raw shot deposits from EPFL ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/23367046)) and DiCarlo-linked work ([4TU](https://data.4tu.nl/datasets/886d8394-adfe-4560-807c-c0fa007110df)). The likeliest event to end the claim is a release of LBNL's eight-transmon QubiC traces, with 25,000 shots per prepared state at 2 GSPS and no data link in either paper ([Jung](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19057), [Le](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33843)). Only 2 of at least 10 new-data papers citing this literature made their traces public ([citation sets](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/ARXIV:2102.12481/citations)), and LBNL-affiliated authors appear on at least four of the rest. An email to that group would test the narrowed claim better than another search, and it could yield the public multi-qubit benchmark the field lacks.
